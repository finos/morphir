# Register: article

For content a person reads to learn or to do something: playbooks, tutorials, orientation pages, guidance.
Reads like a good engineering blog post. Apply [voice.md](./voice.md) throughout.

## Shape

1. **Open with what the reader gets.** One or two sentences: what this page teaches or lets them do, and who it
   is for. A reader arriving from search decides here whether to stay.
2. **Example before theory.** Show the thing working, then explain why it works. A reader who sees the shape of
   the answer forgives a longer explanation after it.
3. **One narrative line.** Order sections as the reader meets the problem, not as the topic decomposes
   taxonomically. Headings are signposts on that line, not a category tree.
4. **End with pointers, not a summary.** Where to go next, what this page deliberately did not cover.

## Tone

- Second person is welcome: "you seal the definition, then run it".
- Contractions are fine.
- Humor is fine when it costs no clarity. One aside per page, not one per paragraph.
- Opinions are allowed and should be owned: "prefer X" with the reason, not "some may prefer X".

## Limits

- When the topic is a flow, a lifecycle, or a structure, show it as a diagram early; see
  [diagrams.md](./diagrams.md). One picture placed before the walkthrough saves the paragraphs that would
  narrate it.
- A code sample must run, or say plainly that it is a sketch.
- Steps a reader will follow get numbered lists and exact commands.
- If a section turns into claim-evidence-tradeoff argument, that content wants the
  [whitepaper](./whitepaper.md) register; move it or link it.
